<section class="chapter" id="chronicle">
 <div class="folio">Leaf II · A chronology</div><h2>Chronicle of the Family</h2><div class="ornament">— ❦ —</div>
 <p class="chapter-intro">Dates below are arranged as a historical timeline. Where the date rests on an estimate or family report, the qualification is retained.</p>
 <div class="timeline">
  <div class="event"><time>Early 1800s · approximate</time><h3>Jan Tylka and Marianna Krzeptowska</h3><p>The earliest couple currently identified in this direct line. The year 1811 has appeared as a possible lead in earlier searching, but is not treated here as a verified birth date.</p></div>
  <div class="event"><time>About 1845–1846 · estimated</time><h3>The birth of Michał / Michael Tylka</h3><p>The estimate is drawn from the reported age in a burial entry from January 1900. A baptismal entry would provide a firmer foundation.</p></div>
  <div class="event"><time>1894 · reported</time><h3>The death of Albina Chlebek</h3><p>Research notes identify Albina Chlebek as Michael Tylka's first wife and place her death in 1894 in the Wielogłowy area. The marriage and burial records should be compared directly.</p></div>
  <div class="event"><time>13 January 1900 · reported burial date</time><h3>A Tylka burial entry in the Wielogłowy area</h3><p>A record reportedly names a Michael Tylka, age 54, with a wife named Magdalena Tylka. Whether this is the same Michael who married Albina, and whether Magdalena was a second wife, remains an open question.</p></div>
  <div class="event"><time>1886</time><h3>Bronisław Michael Tylka Sr. is born</h3><p>Recorded in the family line as the son of Michał/Michael Tylka. This date precedes the reported 1908 migration to America.</p></div>
  <div class="event"><time>1908 · family report</time><h3>Bronisław's journey to Ohio</h3><p>Family research places his immigration from Poland in 1908, with settlement in the St. Clairsville area of Belmont County, Ohio. A possible stop with Tylka relatives in the Pittsburgh or Mount Pleasant region of Pennsylvania remains to be investigated.</p></div>
  <div class="event"><time>1956–2021</time><h3>The lifetime of James Tylka Sr.</h3><p>The grandfather's generation carries the family story into the twentieth and twenty-first centuries.</p></div>
  <div class="event"><time>1986–2016</time><h3>The lifetime of James Tylka Jr.</h3><p>The father's life is part of the immediate family memory preserved by this archive.</p></div>
 </div>
</section>

<section class="chapter" id="places">
 <div class="folio">Leaf III · Places and passage</div><h2>Of Places, Parishes, and Passage</h2><div class="ornament">— ❦ —</div>
 <div class="columns">
  <article class="entry"><div class="num">I · THE OLD COUNTRY</div><h3>Chochołów, Poland</h3><p>A principal place of interest in the search for early Tylka baptisms. The target baptism for Michał/Michael is estimated around 1845–1846. Parish boundaries and neighboring villages should be considered rather than assuming every event occurred in one place.</p></article>
  <article class="entry"><div class="num">II · PARISH RECORDS</div><h3>Wielogłowy and its environs</h3><p>Associated in the research notes with Albina Chlebek's reported 1894 death and a Michael Tylka burial dated 13 January 1900. Original register images and the full entry context are needed to test whether the records refer to the same family.</p></article>
  <article class="entry"><div class="num">III · THE AMERICAN WAYPOINT</div><h3>Pennsylvania</h3><p>The Pittsburgh / Mount Pleasant area is a possible waypoint before Bronisław's settlement in Ohio. The identity of the Tylka relatives who may have received him is not yet established.</p></article>
  <article class="entry"><div class="num">IV · SETTLEMENT</div><h3>St. Clairsville, Belmont County, Ohio</h3><p>Family research identifies this area as Bronisław's destination after arriving in America. Passenger manifests, census records, naturalization papers, local newspapers, and church records may help reconstruct his first years there.</p></article>
 </div>
</section>

<section class="chapter" id="register">
 <div class="folio">Leaf IV · The evidence book</div><h2>Register of Evidence &amp; Testimony</h2><div class="ornament">— ❦ —</div>
 <p class="chapter-intro">A careful genealogical register distinguishes a record that has been examined from an index, a family recollection, or a hypothesis awaiting proof.</p>
 <div class="record"><div class="label">Record A</div><div><h3>Reported burial of Michael Tylka</h3><p>Burial date: 13 January 1900; reported age: 54; location: Wielogłowy area; wife reportedly named Magdalena Tylka.</p><p class="confidence">Status: reported from a record; original image and identity match still to be verified.</p></div></div>
 <div class="record"><div class="label">Record B</div><div><h3>FamilySearch death-record reference</h3><p>A FamilySearch record reference supplied during the research: 6VS9-ZSJD. This identifier is a lead for returning to the record, not a substitute for transcribing and evaluating the original entry.</p><p class="confidence">Status: online record reference supplied; full source details should be captured.</p></div></div>
 <div class="record"><div class="label">Record C</div><div><h3>Reported death of Albina Chlebek</h3><p>Family research places her death in 1894 in Wielogłowy and identifies her as Michael's first wife.</p><p class="confidence">Status: needs comparison with the original death and marriage entries.</p></div></div>
 <div class="record"><div class="label">Record D</div><div><h3>Bronisław's immigration and settlement</h3><p>The year 1908 and the St. Clairsville, Belmont County destination are recorded in the family research. Pennsylvania relatives are a separate, unresolved lead.</p><p class="confidence">Status: family-reported chronology; seek passenger list, census, naturalization, church, and local records.</p></div></div>
 <div class="warning"><h3>A rule of evidence</h3><p>Names and dates in this manuscript are not all supported to the same degree. A record index may contain transcription errors; a family tree may repeat an earlier mistake; and people of the same name may be confused. The strongest conclusions come from original records, multiple independent sources, and a clear explanation of how each source supports the relationship.</p></div>
</section>

<section class="chapter" id="questions">
 <div class="folio">Leaf V · Questions reserved for further inquiry</div><h2>Matters Yet Unresolved</h2><div class="ornament">— ❦ —</div>
 <div class="columns">
  <article class="entry"><div class="num">QUESTION 01</div><h3>Was Michael born in 1845 or 1846?</h3><p>Locate the original baptism register, probably in the 1845–1846 range, and compare the parents' names with the burial record and the known children.</p></article>
  <article class="entry"><div class="num">QUESTION 02</div><h3>Did Michael marry again?</h3><p>Magdalena Wieczorek is a possible second wife after Albina Chlebek's reported death in 1894. A marriage entry and records naming her in the right household would be especially useful.</p></article>
  <article class="entry"><div class="num">QUESTION 03</div><h3>Who were the Pennsylvania relatives?</h3><p>Search passenger lists for contacts or nearest relatives, then compare addresses and names with census, church, and local records in the Pittsburgh / Mount Pleasant area.</p></article>
  <article class="entry"><div class="num">QUESTION 04</div><h3>What can be proven about the Suder line?</h3><p>Research notes identify Michał Suder and Wiktoria Dębska as Antonina Józefa Suder's parents. Seek her baptism, marriage, and family entries to verify the relationship and spelling variants.</p></article>
  <article class="entry"><div class="num">QUESTION 05</div><h3>Is the Semko / Simeon tradition historical?</h3><p>A family message describes an ancestor named Semko or Simeon Tylka, born about 1660, and a story involving Ottoman-era wars and monastery manuscripts. Until independent records corroborate it, this must remain family tradition rather than established descent.</p></article>
  <article class="entry"><div class="num">QUESTION 06</div><h3>Can the earliest generation be extended?</h3><p>Investigate Jan Tylka and Marianna Krzeptowska through marriage, baptism, and death registers. Treat proposed dates such as 1811 as search leads until supported by a record.</p></article>
 </div>
</section>
